# Prompt/context architecture for multi-agent review pipelines

## What do primary sources and official docs say about retrieval/evidence packets, scoped summaries, metadata/fingerprints, semantic maps/cards and keeping full sources available on demand?

### Takeaway

The strongest common pattern is a small, high-signal working context backed by durable external sources: retrieve targeted passages, keep lightweight references and metadata, and reload underlying material when needed. Structured notes and semantic links can improve navigation, but the sources do not establish that a summary, card, fingerprint, or retrieval packet is itself faithful evidence; source fidelity requires immutable source references, inspectable excerpts, and a way to verify the original.

### Cited Findings

- **Retrieval can separate model parameters from an external evidence store.** Lewis et al. define RAG as the combination of a pretrained seq2seq model (parametric memory) with a dense Wikipedia index accessed by a neural retriever (non-parametric memory). One formulation conditions a whole generated sequence on the same retrieved passages; the other can use different passages per token. Their original-author experiments reported state-of-the-art results on three open-domain QA tasks and more specific, diverse, and factual generation than a parametric-only baseline. **Evidence type:** primary research paper, NeurIPS 2020, original-author benchmarks. **Limitations:** this supports retrieved-source conditioning, not a universal evidence-packet schema, citation correctness, counterevidence recall, or code-review performance. [“Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks” (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)

- **A larger context window does not remove the need for selection.** Liu et al. evaluated multi-document QA and key-value retrieval and found that changing the position of relevant information could significantly degrade performance. Performance was often highest when relevant information appeared at the beginning or end and degraded when it was in the middle, including for explicitly long-context models. **Evidence type:** primary research paper with task evaluations; arXiv metadata records TACL acceptance. **Limitations:** the evaluated tasks and 2023-era models do not establish a universal position rule for current models or review pipelines, but they directly caution against treating full-file stuffing as reliable evidence use. [“Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts” (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172)

- **External memory can hold material outside the active window, but management policy remains part of the system.** MemGPT proposes “virtual context management,” inspired by hierarchical operating-system memory, moving information between memory tiers to work beyond a fixed LLM context. The authors evaluated document analysis beyond the underlying window and multi-session chat. **Evidence type:** primary systems paper, original-author evaluations, initially published 2023 and revised 2024. **Limitations:** the work does not prove that retrieved memories preserve all caveats or that autonomous memory management provides independent verification. [“MemGPT: Towards LLMs as Operating Systems” (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08560)

- **Semantic note cards are a documented memory design, not a fidelity guarantee.** A-MEM creates Zettelkasten-inspired notes with structured attributes including contextual descriptions, keywords, and tags, then dynamically indexes and links related memories. It can also update contextual representations and attributes of older notes as new memories arrive. The authors report improvements over prior memory baselines across six foundation models. **Evidence type:** primary research paper, NeurIPS 2025, original-author experiments. **Limitations:** mutable, generated note content is not equivalent to an immutable quotation or source snapshot; the paper does not establish content-hash invalidation or evidentiary sufficiency for review verdicts. [“A-MEM: Agentic Memory for LLM Agents” (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12110)

- **Anthropic’s official context-engineering guidance explicitly recommends progressive disclosure and on-demand loading.** Its “just in time” pattern retains lightweight identifiers such as file paths, stored queries, and web links, then uses tools to load data at runtime. It also identifies names, folder hierarchies, and timestamps as useful metadata, and describes hybrid designs that preload some material while retaining autonomous retrieval. **Evidence type:** vendor engineering guidance published September 29, 2025, based on Anthropic’s product and customer experience. **Limitations:** this is not an independent controlled evaluation; the same article warns that runtime exploration can be slower and can waste context through poor tools, dead ends, or missed information. [“Effective context engineering for AI agents” (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)

- **Scoped summaries need explicit loss control.** Anthropic defines compaction as summarizing a context and starting a new window with that summary. It recommends initially maximizing recall and then improving precision, because overly aggressive compaction can discard subtle context whose importance becomes clear only later. It separately describes structured notes persisted outside the context window and retrieved later. **Evidence type:** vendor engineering guidance and product examples. **Limitations:** “high-fidelity” compaction is an objective to tune, not a guaranteed property; no summary should silently replace the only copy of the evidence. [“Effective context engineering for AI agents” (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)

- **Persistent artifacts plus lightweight references directly address relay loss.** Anthropic’s multi-agent research report recommends letting subagents write structured outputs to external storage and return references to the coordinator, rather than copying every result through successive model contexts. It calls this a way to reduce the “game of telephone,” information loss, and token overhead. Its research architecture also uses a CitationAgent to inspect documents and the report to locate support for claims. **Evidence type:** vendor production-engineering report published June 13, 2025. **Limitations:** the artifact recommendation was not isolated in a controlled fidelity experiment, and a separate citation stage does not itself prove citation accuracy. [“How we built our multi-agent research system” (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)

- **Application-level source fingerprints are distinct from provider cache hashes.** Anthropic documents cumulative hashes of cached prefixes, while OpenAI documents hashing initial prompt tokens for routing. Those mechanisms identify reusable model-computation prefixes; they do not establish that a cited file, excerpt, or semantic card matches the intended source revision. **Evidence type:** official vendor API documentation. **Limitations:** none of the retrieved primary sources supplies a standard application-level evidence fingerprint schema, so content-derived source IDs and freshness checks must be treated as an engineering proposal rather than a research-backed standard. [Anthropic, “Prompt caching” (living documentation, retrieved 2026-10-26)](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching); [OpenAI, “Prompt caching” (living documentation, retrieved 2026-10-26)](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching)

### Inferences

- **Use two layers with different evidentiary status.** Keep immutable source objects and exact locators in the evidence layer; keep generated summaries, embeddings, tags, semantic links, and cards in a replaceable navigation layer. A-MEM supports structured, linked notes as a memory design, while the compaction warning shows why generated representations should not become the sole evidentiary copy. [A-MEM (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12110); [Anthropic context engineering (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)

- **A useful evidence packet should carry provenance and selection context, not just chunks.** Proposed fields are `source_id`, canonical URI/path, source revision, content fingerprint, exact line/byte/page locator, verbatim excerpt, retrieval query or selection rule, retrieval time, and a pointer to the complete source. Keep generated summaries in a separate field with links to their supporting excerpts. This is an architecture inference from RAG’s retrieved passages, just-in-time identifiers, and persistent artifact references, not a schema standardized by those works. [RAG (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401); [Anthropic context engineering (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents); [Anthropic multi-agent research system (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)

- **Make counterevidence a first-class packet output.** Record contrary excerpts, unresolved contradictions, searches performed, coverage limits, and known omissions alongside supporting evidence. This is a proposed safeguard against retrieval and summarization selection bias; the cited retrieval systems do not themselves guarantee complete or balanced evidence. [RAG (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401); [Anthropic context engineering (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)

- **Use source fingerprints for freshness and reproducibility, not as proof of meaning.** A reviewer can fetch the source by reference, recompute its content hash, and compare the cited range with the packet. A changed hash should mark dependent cards and packets stale for new runs, while prior runs retain their pinned source snapshot. This is an engineering inference; the retrieved literature does not experimentally validate this exact mechanism. [Anthropic multi-agent research system (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)

- **Retrieval and prompt caching solve different problems.** Retrieval reduces what must enter the active context; prompt caching reuses computation for a repeated prefix that still remains part of the request context. Long-context position degradation and Gemini’s statement that cached tokens count toward token limits both argue against using caching as a substitute for evidence selection. [“Lost in the Middle” (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172); [Gemini, “Context caching” for GenerateContent (updated 2026-10-23)](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/generate-content/caching)

### Gaps

- No retrieved primary source establishes a standard “evidence packet,” “semantic map,” or “semantic card” contract with mandatory hashes, counterevidence, and independent verification. The concrete contract below is therefore a proposal, not a reported standard.
- No independent replication was collected for RAG, MemGPT, A-MEM, or Anthropic’s artifact and compaction recommendations. The papers provide original-author evaluations; the Anthropic articles provide vendor guidance and production reports.
- The sources do not establish a safe compression ratio or a universal number of chunks/tokens for review. These need task-specific recall, citation, and contradiction tests.
- The research does not determine when an entire file must remain mandatory despite retrieval. Critical-path or high-risk review policies need explicit coverage requirements outside the retrieval ranker.

## What are the actual prompt/context caching contracts of Anthropic, OpenAI and Gemini?

### Takeaway

All three providers reward stable prefixes, but their contracts differ materially and change by model and API generation. Caching is not guaranteed savings: misses, rewrites, minimum lengths, routing, TTL expiry, write premiums, storage charges, ordinary suffix tokens, and output tokens must be measured from actual usage.

### Cited Findings

#### Anthropic Claude

- **Prefix and invalidation contract.** Prompt caching covers the full prefix in `tools`, `system`, then `messages` order through a breakpoint. Explicit writes occur only at marked blocks; changing any block at or before that breakpoint changes the cumulative hash. Reads search previously written entries backward for at most 20 blocks per breakpoint. A request can define up to four breakpoints. Tool-definition changes invalidate the whole hierarchy; other documented settings such as web-search/citation toggles, tool choice, images, thinking, and effort have level-specific or model-specific effects. **Evidence type and limitation:** official living API documentation retrieved 2026-10-26, not an independent evaluation. [Anthropic, “Prompt caching”](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)

- **Automatic does not mean always effective.** Top-level automatic caching marks the last cacheable block and can move forward with a conversation. If that block changes every request, the changing value becomes part of the new prefix hash; lookback cannot invent a cache entry at an earlier stable block that was never written. The documentation’s remedy is an explicit breakpoint after the last stable block. **Evidence type and limitation:** official operational documentation. [Anthropic, “Prompt caching”](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)

- **TTL and concurrency.** The default TTL is five minutes and use refreshes it without an additional refresh charge. The lifetime starts when the reading or writing request starts, so generation time consumes TTL. A one-hour TTL is available at a higher write rate. An entry becomes available only after the first response starts, so parallel requests that all start before that point cannot be assumed to hit. **Evidence type and limitation:** official operational documentation. [Anthropic, “Prompt caching”](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)

- **Pricing is a rate contract, not a savings promise.** The general multipliers are 1.25× base input for a five-minute write, 2× for a one-hour write, and 0.1× for reads. The current table has model exceptions: 0.025× reads for Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1, and 0.05× reads for Claude Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5. Uncached suffix input and output remain separately billable, and multipliers can stack with other pricing modifiers. **Evidence type and limitation:** official pricing table current when retrieved 2026-10-26; model prices and exceptions are volatile. [Anthropic, “Prompt caching”, pricing section](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching#pricing)

- **Usage fields expose actual reads and writes.** `usage.cache_creation_input_tokens` reports tokens written, `usage.cache_read_input_tokens` reports tokens read, and `usage.input_tokens` reports input after the last cache breakpoint. The documented total-input formula is `cache_read_input_tokens + cache_creation_input_tokens + input_tokens`. Both cache fields being zero can indicate that a prompt was not cached, including failure to meet the model-specific minimum. **Evidence type and limitation:** official API documentation. [Anthropic, “Prompt caching”, tracking section](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching#tracking-cache-performance)

- **Eligibility has model-specific limits.** Current documented minima range from 512 to 4,096 tokens depending on model. Requests below the minimum are processed without caching and without an error. Cached thinking read from a prior assistant turn still counts as cached input, and prompt length modifiers still count cached reads and writes toward request length. **Evidence type and limitation:** official API documentation; consult the live per-model table rather than hard-coding one threshold. [Anthropic, “Prompt caching”, limitations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching#cache-limitations)

#### OpenAI

- **The cached object is model KV state for a rendered prefix, not reusable source text.** Reuse requires the entire rendered prefix to match, including provider instructions, developer messages, tools, history, and supported multimodal content. Changes to model, tools, parallel-tool settings, structured-output format, reasoning effort, verbosity, or compaction can affect reuse when they alter that prefix. A session or Agents API relationship does not guarantee a hit. **Evidence type and limitation:** official living API documentation retrieved 2026-10-26. [OpenAI, “Prompt caching”](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching)

- **Current and earlier model contracts must not be conflated.** For GPT-5.6 and later, the minimum cacheable prefix is 1,024 visible input tokens and both implicit and explicit breakpoints are supported. Earlier models use implicit caching only; minimums vary with request settings, and reported cached tokens exclude hidden tokens and round down to a multiple of 128. Explicit-only mode without breakpoints performs no cache write or reuse. Each request can create up to four writes on current models. **Evidence type and limitation:** official model-versioned documentation. [OpenAI, “Prompt caching”, model differences](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching#summary-of-model-differences)

- **A shared prefix is not automatically a cached prefix.** For current models, implicit mode places a breakpoint at the latest eligible message. If a request writes through a dynamic user suffix, a later request with a different suffix cannot use that longer entry as a shorter shared-prefix hit unless an eligible earlier boundary was actually written. OpenAI’s documented remedy is an explicit breakpoint after stable content. **Evidence type and limitation:** official operational guidance. [OpenAI, “Prompt caching”, gotchas](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching#gotchas)

- **Current TTL and pricing.** On GPT-5.6 and later, Responses API `prompt_cache_options.ttl` supports a default minimum lifetime of 30 minutes at a 1.25× total write rate and 12 hours at 2.5×. Reads are 0.1× ordinary input on most of these models and 0.05× on GPT-6.1 Sol. A 30-minute read refreshes coverage to at least 30 minutes; it does not restart a 12-hour clock. Upgrading valid 30-minute content to 12 hours incurs a new 12-hour write without credit for the earlier write. These are minimum lifetimes for pricing eligibility, not a guarantee of reuse or lower latency. **Evidence type and limitation:** official model-versioned pricing and lifetime documentation current at retrieval. [OpenAI, “Prompt caching”, cache lifetime](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching#cache-lifetime-gpt-5-6-and-later)

- **Earlier-model retention and writes differ.** For earlier models, `in_memory` typically remains active for about 5 to 10 minutes of inactivity and up to one hour; `24h` typically keeps entries for about 30 minutes and can retain them for up to 24 hours. The model table says GPT-5.5 and other earlier models have no additional cache-write charge, which still means ordinary input pricing applies rather than free writes. Their cached-input rates are model-dependent. **Evidence type and limitation:** official documentation; “typically” and “up to” are not fixed TTL guarantees. [OpenAI, “Prompt caching”, earlier-model lifetime and model table](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching#cache-lifetime-earlier-models)

- **Routing and isolation affect hits.** Cached states reside on individual machines. Caches are not shared across organizations or regional processing boundaries. On models before GPT-5.6, a stable `prompt_cache_key` helps route related requests but does not pin a machine or guarantee a hit; current models route automatically and can use keys for separate accounting. **Evidence type and limitation:** official operational documentation. [OpenAI, “Prompt caching”, cache location and keys](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching#cache-location)

- **Usage metrics support realized-cost accounting.** The guide lists `usage.input_tokens_details.cached_tokens`, `cache_write_tokens`, and, for supported current models, `cache_write_tokens_details.ttl_30m_tokens` and `ttl_12h_tokens`. Ordinary input is total input minus cached reads minus writes. The documentation explicitly recommends measuring cached tokens, writes, total input, latency, and realized cost. **Evidence type and limitation:** official API documentation; the example dollar prices in the guide are arithmetic illustrations, not universal model prices. [OpenAI, “Prompt caching”, monitoring](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching#monitor-cache-performance)

#### Google Gemini

- **Implicit caching is automatic but conditional.** It is enabled by default for Gemini 2.5 and newer, in stateful and stateless modes, and Google passes on savings when a request hits. Current minimum input thresholds are 4,096 tokens for Gemini 4 Argon, 3.8 Flash, 3.6 Flash, and 3.1 Pro Preview, and 2,048 for Gemini 2.5 Flash and Pro. Google recommends putting large common content first and sending similar prefixes close in time. On Gemini 4 Argon and newer agent loops, caching the full previous request requires at least 2,050 added tokens; otherwise the last 2,050 tokens of the previous request are reprocessed at standard input rate. **Evidence type and limitation:** official Gemini documentation updated 2026-10-23, with model-specific rules. [Gemini, “Context caching” overview](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/caching)

- **Gemini 4 changes the explicit-caching story.** The Interactions API supports implicit caching only. Explicit cache objects are unavailable there and for Gemini 4 Argon onward; supported Gemini 3 and 2.5 models use the GenerateContent API for explicit caching. Explicit caching is labeled Beta and exposed under `v1beta`. **Evidence type and limitation:** official current documentation; do not transfer older GenerateContent assumptions to Gemini 4 or Interactions. [Gemini, “Context caching” overview](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/caching); [Gemini, GenerateContent “Context caching”](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/generate-content/caching)

- **Explicit caches are managed prefix resources.** `caches.create` can store contents and a system instruction for a named model; generation then references `cached_content`. Cached content forms a prompt prefix, and the model does not distinguish cached from ordinary input. Only `ttl` or `expire_time` can be updated, not the cached content. The default TTL is one hour, with no documented minimum or maximum TTL bound. Cached tokens count toward normal token limits and standard GenerateContent rate limits apply. **Evidence type and limitation:** official GenerateContent documentation updated 2026-10-23. [Gemini, GenerateContent “Context caching”](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/generate-content/caching)

- **Explicit cache reads are inspectable, source content is not returned by cache metadata.** The cache service exposes metadata including `name`, `model`, `display_name`, `usage_metadata`, timestamps, and expiry, but the guide says cached content itself cannot be retrieved or viewed through list/get. Cached-token counts appear in cache-service `usage_metadata` and in GenerateContent usage. The implicit-caching overview names `usage.total_cached_tokens` for Python and JavaScript. **Evidence type and limitation:** official documentation; this is not a separate Anthropic-style write-token metric. [Gemini, GenerateContent “Context caching”](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/generate-content/caching); [Gemini, “Context caching” overview](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/caching)

- **Explicit caching has both read and storage economics.** Google bills cached input at a reduced rate when reused, storage according to cached token count and TTL, plus uncached input and output. Although the page uses “cost saving guarantee” wording for explicit mode, its detailed cost section says cached tokens are lower cost “at certain volumes” and adds storage and other charges. The defensible interpretation is a guaranteed applicable cached-input rate on supported explicit reuse, not a guaranteed lower total bill. **Evidence type and limitation:** official vendor pricing/documentation, not an independent cost evaluation. [Gemini, GenerateContent “Context caching”, cost section](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/generate-content/caching#how-explicit-caching-reduces-costs)

- **Current prices must be model- and date-specific.** For example, on 2026-10-26 the pricing page lists Gemini 3.8 Flash Standard at $0.75/M input and $0.075/M cached input plus $0.50/M cached tokens/hour through 2026-12-31, changing to $1.50, $0.15, and $1.00 respectively on 2027-01-01. It lists Gemini 4 Argon Standard at $2/M input and $0.10/M cached input through 2026-11-30, then $4 and $0.20 from 2026-12-01, with implicit caching only. **Evidence type and limitation:** official dynamic pricing page, examples rather than a universal Gemini multiplier; always price the selected model, tier, and date. [Gemini, “Gemini Developer API pricing”](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)

- **No fixed implicit TTL was stated in the retrieved Gemini pages.** The implicit guide recommends requests close in time and exposes hit counts, while the explicit guide defines TTL for managed cache objects. **Evidence type and limitation:** official documentation reviewed; absence of a stated implicit TTL here must not be replaced with an assumed five-minute or one-hour value. [Gemini, “Context caching” overview](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/caching); [Gemini, GenerateContent “Context caching”](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/generate-content/caching)

### Inferences

- **Construct one canonical, byte-stable shared prefix and put variable task data after it.** Keep tool schemas, their ordering, shared rules, output contracts, and stable reference material unchanged. Put lens identity, timestamps, request IDs, mutable evidence selections, and the current question after the reusable breakpoint. This follows all three vendors’ prefix guidance and OpenAI/Anthropic’s explicit warning that a dynamic suffix can prevent creation of the shorter reusable entry. [Anthropic prompt caching](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching); [OpenAI prompt caching](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching); [Gemini context caching](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/caching)

- **“Load common instructions once” should mean once into a canonical artifact and then deterministically reuse that artifact, not assume cross-request memory.** Anthropic and OpenAI requests still present the matching rendered prefix so the provider can find cached computation; Gemini explicit mode instead passes a cache resource reference. Separate lens calls need explicit construction from the same shared artifact. [Anthropic prompt caching](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching); [OpenAI prompt caching](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching); [Gemini GenerateContent caching](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/generate-content/caching)

- **Measure break-even per workload.** Compare total ordinary input, reads, writes by TTL, explicit storage where applicable, suffix tokens, output tokens, and latency against a no-cache run with identical outputs and quality checks. OpenAI’s own example shows that one write plus one full read still has write overhead, while repeated reuse improves the arithmetic; that arithmetic does not guarantee a hit or equal latency. [OpenAI prompt caching](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching); [Anthropic prompt caching](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching); [Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)

- **Do not use cache metrics as evidence-quality metrics.** A high hit rate proves reused computation only. It says nothing about retrieval recall, citation fidelity, contradictory evidence, or reviewer independence, so quality gates must separately inspect source-linked outputs. [OpenAI prompt caching](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching); [Anthropic multi-agent research system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)

- **Warm-up and parallelism need explicit treatment.** Anthropic entries become available after the first response starts, and OpenAI reuse depends on an eligible prior write and machine/routing conditions. A fan-out that starts every lens simultaneously may pay multiple misses or writes; benchmark sequential prewarming where supported rather than assuming all parallel calls share instantly. [Anthropic prompt caching](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching); [OpenAI prompt caching](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching)

### Gaps

- All caching contract and performance claims above are official vendor documentation. No independent controlled caching benchmark was collected, so realized savings and latency for a review pipeline remain unverified.
- Gemini’s retrieved implicit-caching pages do not state a fixed implicit TTL or a detailed invalidation table comparable to Anthropic’s. Do not infer one from another provider.
- Model-specific support, thresholds, retention, and prices are unusually volatile. These notes are a dated snapshot from 2026-10-26, not a durable substitute for checking the selected model’s live documentation.
- Data-retention, Zero Data Retention, regional, and enterprise-variant behavior were not fully compared. Those controls require a separate review before using caches for sensitive client evidence.

## Which multi-agent/decomposition patterns have evidence, and which are merely plausible?

### Takeaway

Sequential decomposition and tool-interleaved reasoning have original-author benchmark evidence in specific tasks, while Anthropic reports strong internal results for breadth-first multi-agent research at much higher token use. Independent review lenses, voting, semantic-card handoffs, and cache-driven savings are plausible adaptations, but the retrieved evidence does not prove their quality or economics in a multi-agent code-review pipeline.

### Cited Findings

- **Prompt chaining is a defined workflow with an explicit tradeoff.** Anthropic describes it as a sequence in which each LLM call consumes the previous call’s output, with optional programmatic gates. It recommends the pattern for cleanly decomposable fixed subtasks and says the intended trade is additional latency for higher accuracy. The same article recommends parallel sectioning/voting, orchestrator-workers, and evaluator-optimizer only where their respective task characteristics fit, and advises adding complexity only after measurement. **Evidence type:** vendor engineering guidance published December 19, 2024, based on customer and product experience. **Limitations:** no controlled prompt-chaining or code-review benchmark is reported in this article. [“Building effective agents” (2024)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)

- **Least-to-most supplies direct but task-limited decomposition evidence.** Zhou et al. first decompose a complex problem into simpler subproblems and then solve them sequentially, supplying previous answers to later steps. Experiments covered symbolic manipulation, compositional generalization, and math reasoning. The abstract reports that GPT-3 code-davinci-002 with 14 exemplars achieved at least 99% accuracy on every tested SCAN split, including length split, versus 16% with chain-of-thought prompting. **Evidence type:** primary paper, ICLR 2023, original-author controlled benchmark comparisons, first published 2022. **Limitations:** SCAN and the other tested tasks are not code review, current models differ, and the result does not measure context cost, source fidelity, or reviewer independence. [“Least-to-Most Prompting Enables Complex Reasoning in Large Language Models” (2022; ICLR 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10625)

- **ReAct supplies evidence for iterative tool grounding, not for multi-agent delegation.** ReAct interleaves reasoning traces with task actions so the model can update plans and query external sources or environments. The authors evaluated HotpotQA, FEVER, ALFWorld, and WebShop. The abstract reports reduced hallucination/error-propagation issues on Wikipedia-backed tasks versus chain-of-thought and absolute success-rate improvements of 34% on ALFWorld and 10% on WebShop over imitation/reinforcement-learning baselines, with one or two in-context examples. **Evidence type:** primary paper, ICLR camera-ready version, original-author experiments, first published 2022. **Limitations:** these results do not show that every ReAct loop improves factual accuracy, and they provide no direct evidence for parallel review lenses or cache savings. [“ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models” (2022; ICLR 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)

- **Anthropic’s subagent result is substantial but vendor-internal and domain-specific.** Its lead-agent/parallel-subagent research system outperformed single-agent Claude Opus 4 by 90.2% on Anthropic’s internal research evaluation. The report says the approach especially fits breadth-first queries with independent directions. It also reports that token usage alone explained 80% of performance variance in its BrowseComp analysis, while agents used about 4× chat tokens and multi-agent systems about 15× chat tokens. **Evidence type:** vendor production report and internal evaluation published June 13, 2025. **Limitations:** this is not an independent evaluation, the comparator and tasks are research-specific, and token expenditure is a major confound and cost rather than evidence of efficiency. [“How we built our multi-agent research system” (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)

- **Separate contexts help compression and separation of concerns, not automatic agreement or truth.** Anthropic says subagents explore in separate context windows with distinct prompts, tools, and trajectories, then return condensed findings. Its context-engineering article gives a typical distilled return of 1,000 to 2,000 tokens after much larger exploration. It also says tasks requiring all agents to share the same context or having many dependencies may be poor multi-agent fits, with most coding tasks offering less clean parallelism than research. **Evidence type:** vendor engineering reports. **Limitations:** condensed handoffs can omit counterevidence, and independent context windows alone do not establish statistical or epistemic independence. [“How we built our multi-agent research system” (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system); [“Effective context engineering for AI agents” (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)

- **Delegation needs explicit boundaries.** Anthropic reports that vague subagent instructions caused duplicated searches, gaps, and divergent interpretations. It recommends an objective, output format, tool/source guidance, and clear task boundaries for every subagent. Its production remedies also include effort budgets, tracing, deterministic checkpoints, and human evaluation for subtle source-selection failures. **Evidence type:** vendor production experience. **Limitations:** these are operational lessons rather than isolated causal effects from controlled ablations. [“How we built our multi-agent research system” (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)

### Inferences

- **Evidence-backed within limited original tasks:** least-to-most supports sequential decomposition, and ReAct supports interleaved reasoning/tool use. Their evidence justifies controlled trials in review, not a claim of established review superiority. [Least-to-Most (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10625); [ReAct (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)

- **Vendor-production-supported but not independently established:** orchestrator-worker subagents for broad, parallel research and persistent artifact handoffs have a credible production report, including a large internal quality gain and a large token-cost warning. Transfer to code review remains a hypothesis, especially because Anthropic itself notes coding’s dependencies. [Anthropic multi-agent research system (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)

- **Plausible but requiring direct review-pipeline evaluation:** independent security/correctness/data lenses, voting as a defect detector, semantic maps as review handoffs, and cached shared rubrics as a net-cost optimization. Anthropic’s general parallelization guidance and caching contracts motivate these designs but do not demonstrate their combined effect. [Building effective agents (2024)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents); [OpenAI prompt caching](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching)

- **Do not equate more tokens or more agents with better verification.** Anthropic’s own variance analysis makes token budget a major explanatory factor. A fair comparison should report quality against total tokens, tool calls, latency, and dollars, and include a single-agent baseline given a comparable opportunity to inspect evidence. [Anthropic multi-agent research system (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)

### Gaps

- No independent, controlled head-to-head evaluation of single-agent versus independent-lens multi-agent code review was found in the scoped source set.
- No source quantifies counterevidence loss caused specifically by condensed subagent summaries or semantic cards.
- No independent replication of Anthropic’s 90.2% internal research result or its token-variance analysis was collected.
- Prompt chaining and voting lack review-specific evidence here for false-positive rate, false-negative rate, and total cost. Those remain test requirements rather than established benefits.

## How can shared common instructions be loaded once, lenses kept independent, and repeated full-file prompts avoided without hiding counterevidence?

### Takeaway

Build every lens from the same canonical instruction artifact and immutable source catalog, but vary only a scoped task suffix and do not expose peer conclusions before each lens submits. Replace repeated raw-file prompts with source-linked evidence packets and on-demand reads, then have a coordinator or independent verifier reopen exact source ranges and actively check contradictions; treat caching as an optional measured optimization, never as the fidelity mechanism.

### Cited Findings

- **Stable-prefix guidance directly supports a shared instruction layer.** Anthropic and OpenAI recommend static tools/instructions/reference material before changing content, with explicit breakpoints where needed. OpenAI also recommends append-only history and stable tool ordering/schemas, while warning that summarization or compaction can reset prefix reuse. **Evidence type and limitation:** official vendor operational guidance, not a quality evaluation. [Anthropic prompt caching](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching); [OpenAI prompt caching](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching)

- **Just-in-time references directly support avoiding repeated full-file prompts.** Anthropic recommends lightweight identifiers and metadata, progressive disclosure, and hybrid preload/retrieval strategies, while warning about latency and missed information. **Evidence type and limitation:** vendor engineering guidance. [Anthropic context engineering (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)

- **External artifacts directly support lightweight subagent handoffs.** Anthropic recommends storing specialized outputs externally and passing references to the coordinator to reduce copying and relay loss. Its delegation guidance requires bounded objectives, tools/sources, and output formats. **Evidence type and limitation:** vendor production guidance, not independent proof of fidelity. [Anthropic multi-agent research system (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)

- **Independent lenses need a later synthesis barrier by design.** Anthropic describes subagents with separate contexts and distinct trajectories, with the lead synthesizing their returned findings. Its evaluator guidance separately values factual accuracy, citation accuracy, completeness, source quality, and human checks for subtle source-selection bias. **Evidence type and limitation:** vendor architecture and evaluation guidance; it does not by itself prove independence or completeness. [Anthropic multi-agent research system (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)

- **Summaries cannot be the only verification path.** Anthropic warns that compaction can discard subtle but critical details and recommends recall-first tuning. RAG and ReAct instead demonstrate ways to condition on or actively obtain external evidence. **Evidence type and limitation:** vendor guidance plus original-author task evaluations; none supplies a complete anti-selection-bias protocol for review. [Anthropic context engineering (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents); [RAG (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401); [ReAct (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)

### Inferences

1. **Create the canonical shared layer once per run/version, then instantiate every lens from it.** Include common review rules, evidence-status definitions, citation/output schema, tool contracts, and non-negotiable source-fidelity requirements. Give this artifact a version and content hash. Render it byte-identically at the start of every lens request, with any provider cache breakpoint immediately after it. Put the lens name, assigned question, partition, run timestamps, and other mutable data after that boundary. This is a proposed implementation of the vendors’ stable-prefix contracts, not a claim that subagents automatically inherit a coordinator’s context. [Anthropic prompt caching](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching); [OpenAI prompt caching](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching); [Anthropic context engineering (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)

2. **Maintain an immutable source catalog and a separate semantic navigation layer.** For each source version, store the full content or an access-controlled pointer plus canonical path/URI, revision, content hash, parser/version where relevant, and durable locators. Semantic cards may contain a generated summary, entities/symbols, tags, embedding keys, related-card links, and source references, but must be labeled derived and rebuildable. A changed source hash marks the card stale for new work; it must not rewrite the evidence snapshot cited by an old run. This proposal combines A-MEM’s structured links with persistent artifacts while avoiding their evidentiary conflation. [A-MEM (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12110); [Anthropic multi-agent research system (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)

3. **Issue scoped evidence packets, not opaque summaries.** A proposed packet contract is:
   - `source`: source ID, canonical URI/path, revision, full-content hash, and full-source access reference;
   - `excerpt`: verbatim text, exact locator, excerpt hash, and parser/rendering details needed to reproduce it;
   - `selection`: lens/query, retrieval or static-selection method, corpus/snapshot ID, and selection time;
   - `analysis`: clearly labeled generated summary or claim, with excerpt references and confidence/status;
   - `adversarial`: contrary excerpts, unresolved conflicts, queries/checks performed, known coverage gaps, and `none_found` versus `not_checked` distinguished.

   The packet should include enough verbatim context to interpret each excerpt, while allowing a reviewer to fetch the complete source on demand. This is a proposed schema derived from retrieval, progressive disclosure, and artifact guidance, not a standard validated by the cited papers. [RAG (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401); [Anthropic context engineering (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents); [Anthropic multi-agent research system (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)

4. **Preserve lens independence until artifacts are frozen.** Give each lens the common instructions and its scoped packet, but not another lens’s draft, verdict, or selected “consensus” evidence. Record its output as an immutable artifact with source references. Only then should the coordinator compare findings, merge duplicates, and adjudicate disagreements. For important claims, a separate verification step should reopen the raw source by hash/locator, test whether the excerpt supports the claim, and run targeted contrary-evidence searches beyond the supporting packet. This is a review-specific inference from separate-context subagents, citation evaluation, and compaction-risk guidance. [Anthropic multi-agent research system (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system); [Anthropic context engineering (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)

5. **Evaluate fidelity and economics together.** Compare at least: a single-agent full-context baseline; shared-prefix plus evidence packets; and independent lenses plus synthesis. Use planted contradictions, subtle caveats, changed-source fingerprints, unretrieved relevant passages, and stale semantic cards as test cases. Measure source/citation accuracy, counterevidence recall, defect false positives/negatives, coverage, ordinary/read/write tokens, storage, total dollars, tool calls, and latency. Prompt-cache hit rate is only an operational metric, and multi-agent quality must be reported alongside Anthropic’s demonstrated risk of much higher total token use. [Anthropic multi-agent research system (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system); [OpenAI prompt caching](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching); [Anthropic prompt caching](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)

### Gaps

- The proposed packet schema, hash-invalidation policy, and synthesis barrier have not been empirically validated in this research; they are engineering recommendations derived from the cited evidence.
- The sources do not define a reliable automatic test for epistemic independence. Shared models, training data, retrieval indexes, and prompts can still correlate lens errors even when contexts are separate.
- No universal policy establishes how much verbatim context an excerpt needs or when full-file review is mandatory. High-risk coverage rules and human review remain open requirements.
- Cross-host behavior for actually loading a common artifact into isolated subagents was not tested. The architecture must verify the active host’s dispatch/context contract rather than assume inheritance, caching, or shared filesystem access.
